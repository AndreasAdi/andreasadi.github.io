---
title: AI Is Our Attempt to Speak Like God
date: 2026-10-06
summary: The trajectory of AI points toward a world where saying words makes things exist. That is the oldest picture of God we have.
---

The first thing God does in the Bible is speak. "Let there be light," and there
was light. There are no tools in that sentence and no materials, and no steps
between the wanting and the having. The word is enough.

The psalmist says it plainly: "He spoke, and it came to be; he commanded, and
it stood firm" (Psalm 33:9). John opens his gospel the same way: "In the
beginning was the Word." For as long as people have tried to picture what it
means to be God, the picture has been this. You say it, and it is.

I have been thinking about that picture a lot lately, because of what I do all
day.

## The gap is closing

For most of my life as a programmer, my job was translation. Someone had an
idea in plain words, and I turned it into syntax a machine could follow. The
distance between "I want a page that shows my orders" and a page that shows
orders was weeks of my time, and that distance was the whole profession.

That distance is collapsing. Today I can describe a feature in a sentence and
watch it get written. You can describe a picture and get the picture, or a
song, or a short film. Agents take a paragraph and come back with a working
app. Each year the sentence gets shorter, the result gets bigger, and the part
in the middle, the part that used to be human labour, gets thinner.

Follow that line forward and it is not hard to see where it goes. Language
models already drive tools. Tools already drive machines. Machines already
print, cut, assemble and build. Connect them well enough and one day someone
says "build a house here, with three bedrooms and a lot of light," and a house
begins to exist.

Say a thing and it appears. We are building the oldest image of God we have,
out of silicon.

## We have always wanted this

None of this is a new desire. It is the oldest one.

In the garden the serpent's offer was not money or pleasure. It was "you will be
like God" (Genesis 3:5). At Babel people said "come, let us build ourselves a
city, with a tower that reaches to the heavens, so that we may make a name for
ourselves" (Genesis 11:4). God's answer to Babel is a line that reads
differently in 2026: "nothing they plan to do will be impossible for them"
(Genesis 11:6).

Every tool we ever made shortened the distance between wanting and having. Fire,
the plough, the printing press, the compiler. AI is the first tool aimed straight
at the last step, the one where you only have to say it. It is not hard to see
why it feels like reaching for something that was never ours.

## But it is not the same thing

When I look closely, though, the imitation is easy to see.

When God speaks, Hebrews says, "what is seen was not made out of what was
visible" (Hebrews 11:3). That is creation from nothing. When I speak to a model,
nothing like that happens. My words go into a machine trained on billions of
other people's words, which runs on electricity from a power plant, on chips
made from sand somebody dug up. Every "creation" is a rearrangement of things
that already existed. Our word does not make. It only directs.

So we are not becoming gods. We are doing what children do when they put on
their father's shoes and walk around the house. The shoes are real, and the
walking is real, but nobody is fooled about whose shoes they are.

There is a better way to read this, and I think it is the true one. If people
are made in the image of a God who creates, then of course we create. Tolkien
called it sub-creation: we make, he wrote, "by the law in which we're made." A
desire to make things by speaking is not proof that we are rebelling. It might
just be proof of whose image we carry. The question is what we do with it.

## The danger is in the speaker

When the cost of making something drops to the cost of a sentence, the sentence
is all that is left. The skill stops mattering and the intention becomes
everything.

Jesus said that "the mouth speaks what the heart is full of" (Luke 6:45). For
most of history there was a lot of friction between the heart and the world.
You might want something cruel and never have the means, the time or the
patience to build it. That friction was a kind of mercy. Remove it and whatever
is in us comes out faster and at scale, the good and the rest.

When God spoke, what came out was called good, again and again, all through the
first chapter of Genesis. When we speak, what comes out is whatever we are. Babel
did not end because the tower was too tall. It ended because of why it was being
built.

## Where I land

I do not think AI will make us gods. I think it shows, more clearly than
anything before it, how much we have always wanted to be.

I am not against it. I use these tools every day and I am grateful for them. But
if we are walking toward a world where words become things, then words carry a
weight they never had before. Careless speech used to waste breath. Soon it may
build something.

So I want to be careful what I say. Not because the machine is listening, but
because, for the first time, saying and making are becoming the same act. Only
one Speaker has ever been good enough to be trusted with that. The rest of us
should probably start practising humility before we get the full power.
